## Department of Tourism — PDF page 36 — program description

Tourism represents a social phenomenon centered on human mobility. Tourism science encompasses systematically studying complex human activities and social phenomena within the tourism domain. The discipline established its academic foundations through economic approaches parallel to tourism industry growth. The field's evolution into an interdisciplinary domain, incorporating resource development, tourism management, and marketing, reflects increasing tourism accessibility and national welfare policy emphasis. As a multidisciplinary field, tourism science transcends the mere application of peripheral sciences, including economics, business administration, sociology, psychology, and geography, utilizing these disciplines as analytical frameworks for understanding tourism phenomena. The tourism industry contributes more than 10% to global GDP, employment, and investment indicators, with projections indicating doubled growth within the next decade. The sector generates higher value addition than the automotive, steel, and electronics industries, leading to increased tourism-focused national policies worldwide. The expansion of Korea's tourism industry, mainly influenced by Chinese economic growth, has generated a growing demand for tourism professionals possessing a comprehensive interdisciplinary understanding of the field.
