# School of Economics and Finance — courses

## School of Economics and Finance — PDF page 47 — course: Microeconomy 1: Consumers and Businesses

The course presents fundamental microeconomic principles through an optimization perspective, examining consumer utility maximization, producer cost minimization, and perfectly competitive market dynamics.

## School of Economics and Finance — PDF page 47 — course: Macroeconomics 1: National Income and Prices

The course examines theoretical frameworks underlying macroeconomic variables, including national income, interest rates, unemployment, inflation, and international indices. It analyzes national income concepts, IS-LM models, aggregate supply-demand relationships, classical models, consumption-investment theories, monetary dynamics, and policy frameworks.

## School of Economics and Finance — PDF page 47 — course: Econometrics

The course develops advanced statistical analysis capabilities for interpreting economic phenomena, examining regression models, specification analysis, heteroscedasticity, and time-series correlation, and incorporating practical economic data analysis through statistical applications.

## School of Economics and Finance — PDF page 47 — course: Financial Economics

The course covers the most fundamental topics in finance and investment theory. Topics include the time value of money, present and future value calculations, net present value and internal rate of return, capital budgeting, portfolio theory, capital asset pricing models, capital structure, and dividend policy.
