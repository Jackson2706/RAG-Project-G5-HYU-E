# Appendix 2: apostille certification and consular authentication

## Appendix 2: apostille certification and consular authentication (PDF page 31)

Applicants with academic history from overseas institutions must submit an official diploma or degree and official academic transcript verified by apostille or a Korean embassy within the designated period.

The source explains that Korea became the 90th contracting state of the Apostille Convention, effective July 14, 2007. An apostille allows a document issued by a signatory country to be certified for legal use in other signatory states, replacing complex consular authentication procedures. Apostilled documents have the same effect as documents verified by diplomatic offices under this explanation.

For graduates from Apostille Convention member countries, check the relevant national authority on the Hague Conference on Private International Law (HCCH) website. The illustrated procedure is: school issues document → visit apostille-issuing organization → apply for apostille → evaluation → apostille issued.

For graduates from universities in non-member countries, obtain consular verification from a Republic of Korea embassy or consulate in the respective country.
